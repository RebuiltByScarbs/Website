Why I coach this

I put on 30kg before I built this.

Not gradually, either. A run of injuries and a motivation crash later, I was further from "in shape" than I'd ever been — despite years of training experience telling me exactly what I was supposed to do.

[Replace the icon above with a real training photo of you — not a posed headshot.]

Here's the part that actually matters: knowing what to do was never the problem. I already knew how training worked.

What I didn't have was a system for the version of me that had just been through an injury, lost the routine, and didn't trust his own consistency any more. Every plan I found assumed I was starting from zero, or assumed motivation would just show up on its own. Neither was true.

The gap wasn't knowledge. It was structure for exactly this situation — coming back, not starting out.

Rebuilding it properly — training back up deliberately, eating in a way that didn't require white-knuckle discipline, and doing the identity work nobody talks about — is what became The Rebuild. I built the process I needed, then started running other people through it.

If you've got a few years of training behind you and you're standing exactly where I was, I'm not going to talk to you like a beginner. You already know how to train. You need the comeback system, not another basics course.

Lived it, not just studied it

The programming is backed by research — but it's shaped by actually going through the setback, not just reading about it.

No patronising

You've trained before. Coaching should sound like it, not like a beginner's guide with your name on it.

Structure over willpower

Motivation isn't the plan. A setback protocol, decided in advance, is what actually holds up.
