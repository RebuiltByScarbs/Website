Fibre & micronutrients — don't skip this

Aim for 2-3 handfuls of vegetables daily minimum, plus fruit. This isn't about calories — it's fibre, micronutrients, and satiety while you're in a deficit. Broccoli, spinach, peppers, mixed salad, berries all count and barely touch your calorie budget.

Total daily calories and protein matter far more than exact timing — but these details make adherence and recovery easier, which is where most people actually fall down.

01
Spread protein across 3-4 mealsAim for roughly 0.3-0.4g/kg bodyweight per meal. Muscle protein synthesis responds to protein in a per-meal dose-dependent way up to a point — one huge protein meal a day is less effective than spreading it out.
02
Eat carbs around trainingDoesn't have to be exact, but having a carb-containing meal within a few hours either side of training supports performance and recovery, especially on higher-volume days.
03
Meal frequency is a preference, not a rule3 meals or 6 — whatever fits your schedule and appetite regulation. There's no meaningful metabolic advantage to more frequent smaller meals once total protein and calories are matched.
04
Don't under-eat earlier in the day "to save calories for later"This is the fastest route to an uncontrolled binge at 9pm. Structure your calories across the day the way you'll actually be able to hold to.

This is where most people's nutrition actually falls apart — not on a random Tuesday, but on a Saturday night or a work dinner. Plan for it instead of pretending it won't happen. The weekend makes up nearly 30% of the week, don't let this be your downfall.

Cocktail~200-350+

Alcohol calories don't build muscle or fill you up much, and alcohol itself slightly suppresses fat oxidation while it's being processed. Practical approach: budget for it in your daily/weekly calories rather than "being good" all week and blowing it entirely on Saturday, and favour spirits with a diet mixer or a glass of wine over a round of cocktails if you're trying to stay closer to target.

Eating out without derailing the week

  • Pick a protein-forward main (grilled, not battered/breaded) and ask for extra veg instead of extra carbs if you're tracking closely.
  • Don't arrive starving — a small protein snack beforehand stops you ordering three courses out of hunger.
  • One meal, even a genuinely indulgent one, isn't going to ruin the whoel week. The damage comes from the guilt-spiral afterwards, not the meal itself.

Your calculated numbers are a starting point, not gospel. Real bodies don't perfectly match equations — here's how to actually adjust.

01
Give it 2-3 weeks before touching anythingDaily weight swings 1-2kg from water, sodium, and hormones alone. You need a trend, not a data point. Weighing yourself more frequently allows you to see a clear trend to the data, instead of a 'high' or 'low' day.
02
Compare weekly averages, not single daysIf your weekly average isn't moving in the expected direction after 2-3 weeks of genuinely consistent tracking, it's time to adjust — not before.
03
Adjust by 5-10%, not by slashing caloriesIf fat loss has stalled, drop calories by roughly 100-150kcal (mostly from carbs/fat, keep protein up) rather than panicking and cutting 500kcal overnight.
04
Re-test after another 2-3 weeksSame process. This is a repeating loop, not a one-time calculation — your maintenance calories will also drop as your bodyweight drops, so periodic recalculation is expected, not a sign something's wrong.
Why does this calculator give different numbers to other fitness apps?
Different apps use different equations and activity assumptions. Mifflin-St Jeor (used here) is generally considered one of the more accurate predictive equations in the research for most people — but it's still an estimate. Treat the number as a starting point to adjust from, not a fixed truth.
Do I need to hit my macros exactly every single day?
No. Being within 5-10g on protein and reasonably close on calories most days matters far more than hitting an exact number every day. Consistency over weeks beats precision on any single day.
I'm not losing weight even though I'm "in a deficit" — what's going on?
This comes from almost always under-reporting intake (portion sizes, cooking oil, drinks, weekend food not logged) or overestimating activity level. Before assuming your metabolism is broken, tighten up tracking accuracy for two honest weeks.
Should I do a "cheat day"?
Planned flexibility built into your weekly calories works better long-term than an unplanned blowout followed by guilt and restriction. See the Alcohol & eating out section — budget for it, don't "earn" or "punish" with food. When selecting 'off plan' meals, prioritse food you know digests well - not a greasy kebab!
